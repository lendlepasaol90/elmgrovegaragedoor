Is it time

When a new garage door makes more sense than repair

Plenty of garage doors can be kept running for years with good repairs, and we will always tell you when that is the smarter choice. There comes a point, though, where repairs stop being good value. A single layer steel door from the 1980s with several dented sections, rusted bottom edges and original springs is a good example. Each fix keeps it going a little longer, but the door still lets cold air pour in and still looks tired. At that stage, putting money into a new door usually gets you far more for every dollar spent than another round of patching.

Other reasons to replace are less about wear and more about how you use the garage. If you have turned part of it into a workshop or home gym, an insulated door makes the space usable in winter. If the garage is attached and shares a wall with a bedroom or family room, a better insulated door helps keep those rooms comfortable. Noise is another factor. Modern doors with nylon rollers and well designed hinges run far more quietly than many older doors, which matters when the garage sits under a bedroom.

Then there is curb appeal. On many homes the garage door is the largest single feature facing the street, sometimes taking up a third of the front elevation. Swapping a plain flat panel door for one with a carriage house pattern, a long panel design or a row of windows can change the whole character of a ranch or Colonial. If you are preparing to sell, or simply tired of looking at a faded, dented door every day, a new one is one of the most visible updates you can make.

  • Cold, drafty garage in winter
  • Noisy, rough operation every day

Choosing a door

Picking the right door for your home and our winters

Choosing a door comes down to a few key decisions: material, insulation, design, color and windows. Steel is the most common choice for good reason, since it is durable, low maintenance and available in a huge range of styles. Wood offers a warm, natural look but needs regular finishing in Wisconsin weather. Wood composite and fiberglass overlay doors give a wood grain appearance with less upkeep. Aluminum and glass full view doors suit modern homes. We walk you through the tradeoffs so you can pick what fits how you live, not just what looks good in a brochure.

Insulation deserves real thought in our climate. Doors range from single layer steel with no insulation, to two layer doors with polystyrene backing, to three layer sandwich doors with steel on both sides and either polystyrene or injected polyurethane foam in the middle. Polyurethane bonds to the steel skins, which adds strength and dent resistance as well as higher R value. For an attached garage, a heated workshop or a garage under living space, a well insulated door is often worth the extra investment. We can show you how each construction looks in cross section so the difference is easy to see.

Design choices include the panel style, such as short raised panels, long raised panels, flush panels or carriage house designs, along with color and window options. Windows can sit in the top section for natural light without giving up privacy, and glass choices range from clear to frosted to decorative inserts. Hardware like handles and strap hinges can complete a carriage house look. We bring samples and help you picture the result on your house, taking into account the roofline, trim, siding and brick color. Seeing a sample against your actual siding beats guessing from a screen.

Warning signs

Signs you are ready for a new door

01

Repairs keep adding up

If you have paid for springs, cables, a section and rollers over a few years and something else is now failing, the total starts to approach the cost of a new door. At that point a replacement often gives better value and fewer service calls going forward.

02

Door lets in cold air

Light around the edges, drafts at the bottom, or frost on the inside of the panels in winter all suggest the door is not sealing or insulating well. An insulated door with fresh seals can make an attached garage noticeably warmer and protect the rooms next to it.

03

Rust or rot spreading

Rust that has eaten through the bottom of a steel door, or rot that has softened the lower sections of a wood door, rarely stays contained. Once corrosion or decay reaches several sections, replacing individual panels stops making financial sense compared with a new door.

04

Door feels heavy or rough

A door that groans, shakes or struggles even after maintenance may have worn out hardware throughout. When the sections, tracks and springs are all aging together, a complete new system with matched parts often runs better than an old door full of mixed repairs.

05

Outdated or damaged look

Faded paint, mismatched replacement panels and dents that cannot be removed make the whole house look older. If you are updating siding, windows or the roof, or preparing to sell, a new garage door is one of the most noticeable improvements from the street.

06

Safety concerns

Older doors may have extension springs without containment cables, pinch prone joints, or other features that do not meet current expectations for safety. If the door feels unsafe to operate around kids or pets, replacing it with a modern design is worth discussing.

Installation day

How we install your new garage door

Everything starts with accurate measurements. Before ordering, we measure the opening width and height, the headroom above the opening, the sideroom on each side, and the backroom from the opening to the back wall. We also look at the framing around the opening, the condition of the jambs and header, and any obstructions like ductwork, lights or storage racks that might interfere with the tracks. These numbers decide which track system and spring arrangement your door needs, and catching a problem now avoids surprises on installation day. We double check every number before the order goes in.

Removing the old door comes first, and it is one of the more hazardous parts of the job. The existing springs must be safely unwound before the door can be taken apart, especially older extension springs that may lack safety cables. We take the old door down section by section, remove the old tracks and hardware, and inspect the framing. If the jambs are rotted or the header is in rough shape, we let you know right away and discuss the options before installing the new door against a weak surface.

The new door goes in section by section, starting with the bottom section set level on the floor. Tracks are mounted plumb and parallel, rear hangers are anchored into solid framing, and the torsion spring assembly is installed with springs sized for the new door's actual weight and height. We set the cables evenly, wind the springs to the correct turns, and balance the door by hand. New weather seal goes on the bottom and along the perimeter, and the opener is connected, adjusted and tested with the safety sensors and auto reverse.

After the install

Getting the most from your new garage door

Before we leave, we walk you through the door. You will see how the manual release works, how to lock the door if it has a lock, and how to tell if the balance is right by lifting it halfway with the opener disengaged. We show you where the safety sensors are and why they need to stay aligned and clean. If you have questions about the opener remote, keypad or any smart features, we take the time to set them up and explain them before you are left on your own.

Basic care keeps a new door in good shape. Rinse the door a couple of times a year, especially after winter, to remove road salt and grime from the bottom section. Keep the bottom seal clear of leaves, ice and snow so it can seal properly against the floor. Lubricate hinges, roller bearings and springs once or twice a year with a garage door lubricant, but leave the inside of the tracks clean and dry. Watch for loose fasteners, frayed cables or unusual noises and call us if something seems off.

Painted steel doors generally need little beyond cleaning, though touching up a scratch keeps rust from starting. Wood doors need regular inspection and refinishing on a schedule that depends on sun exposure and finish type. Composite and fiberglass overlay doors fall in between. Whatever material you choose, the biggest factors in how long a door lasts are proper installation, correct spring balance and simple attention over the years. A door that is balanced and maintained will treat your opener better too. We are always happy to answer questions after the job, too.

Pricing, explained

What affects the cost

  1. 01

    Door size

    A single car door costs less than a double door, and taller doors for trucks or campers cost more than standard heights. Larger doors also need heavier springs and longer tracks, which adds to the hardware portion of the project.

  2. 02

    Material and insulation

    Single layer steel is the most economical. Insulated two and three layer steel doors cost more, with polyurethane cores at the higher end. Wood, wood composite, fiberglass overlay and full view aluminum and glass doors each fall in their own price ranges.

  3. 03

    Design and windows

    Carriage house designs, decorative hardware, custom colors and window sections add cost. Decorative glass and insulated glass cost more than plain glazing. Simple flush or raised panel doors in standard colors are the most budget friendly design choices.

  4. 04

    Site and opener work

    Low headroom track systems, repairs to rotted jambs or headers, and electrical changes add to the job. If your opener is old or not compatible with a heavier insulated door, replacing it at the same time is often worth considering.

How long does a new garage door installation take?

Most single and double door installations are completed in one day, often in about half a day once we are on site. Larger doors, low headroom systems, framing repairs or opener replacement can extend the time. The wait for the door itself is usually longer than the installation, since many doors are built to order in a specific size, color and design.

Do I need a new opener with a new door?

Not always. If your opener is in good condition, has working safety sensors and is strong enough for the new door, we can reconnect and adjust it. A heavier insulated door, an opener more than fifteen or twenty years old, or one missing modern safety features may justify a replacement. We will assess your opener and tell you honestly what we recommend.

What insulation level should I choose?

It depends on how you use the garage. For a detached garage used only for parking, basic insulation or none may be fine. For attached garages, especially those under or next to living space, or garages used as workshops in Elm Grove winters, a three layer door with polyurethane or thick polystyrene insulation makes a noticeable difference in comfort. Many homeowners notice the difference the first cold week.

Will you remove my old garage door?

Yes. Removal and haul away of the old door, tracks and springs are part of our installation. We safely unwind the old springs, take the door down section by section, and load everything onto our truck. We also clean up packaging and debris from the new door, leaving your garage and driveway the way we found them. Nothing is left behind for you to deal with.

Can a new door fit an older or unusual opening?

Usually, yes. Doors come in many widths and heights, and track systems can be adjusted for limited headroom or tight sideroom. If the opening is a nonstandard size, a custom sized door may be needed. In some cases, minor framing changes make a standard door fit better. We measure carefully and explain the options before you order anything. No guessing, no forcing a poor fit.

How do I choose a style that suits my house?

Start with your home's architecture. Ranches and midcentury homes often look good with clean flush or long panel designs, while Colonials and traditional homes suit raised panels or carriage house styles. Consider color relative to your trim and siding, and whether windows will match the home's front windows. We bring samples and photos to help you compare options. You can also see doors on similar homes around the village.
